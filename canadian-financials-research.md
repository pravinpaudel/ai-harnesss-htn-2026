**Headline results:**
Royal Bank of Canada delivered record net income of $6.0 billion, up 11% year-over-year, with diluted EPS of $4.23 (adjusted $4.28), up 13% and 11% respectively. Revenue of $18.54 billion exceeded the LSEG consensus of $18.07-18.14 billion. Pre-provision, pre-tax earnings continued to expand, underscoring strong operating leverage across the franchise. **Segment performance:**
Capital Markets delivered a standout quarter with earnings rising 16% year-over-year, benefiting from robust advisory and underwriting activity. Insurance results moderated relative to the prior year.

**Sector-specific metrics:**
Net interest margin was down 3-4 basis points sequentially, reflecting the normalization of seasonally higher lending spreads from the prior quarter and the impact of certain Capital Markets transactions. On a year-over-year basis, NIM remained relatively stable. Non-interest income rose 13%, driven by fee-based revenue growth in Wealth Management and Capital Markets.

**Margin & profitability:**
Return on equity was 17.9% (some reports cite 18.1%), up approximately 60 basis points year-over-year. The Canadian Banking segment achieved a leading efficiency ratio of 36.7-37%. The overall bank efficiency ratio continued its improving trajectory, reflecting HSBC cost synergies and disciplined expense management.

**Balance sheet & capital:**
CET1 ratio stood at 13.5%, well above regulatory requirements, providing substantial capital flexibility. The bank repurchased 5.6 million shares for $1.6 billion while paying $2.4 billion in common dividends (total payout ratio of 69%). Provision for credit losses on loans totaled $1,000 million (36 bps of average loans), with PCL on impaired loans at $979 million (35 bps) and performing loan provisions minimal at $21 million (1 bp).

**Management commentary:**
Management highlighted the bank's diversified business model as a key strength, noting that strong performance in capital-light businesses (Wealth, Capital Markets) offset any pressures in rate-sensitive segments. CEO Dave McKay noted that tariffs are affecting some sectors, but consumer spending remains solid and many business lines continue to grow. The bank said it has already reached several targets set at its Investor Day approximately 18 months ago, including stronger ROE and better efficiency ratios.

**Guidance:**
RBC remains cautiously optimistic on the Canadian economy, expecting mild growth supported by prior rate cuts, trade diversification, and targeted fiscal measures. Management flagged tariff uncertainty as the primary macro risk, noting headwinds for commercial clients in trade-exposed sectors while maintaining confidence in the bank's ability to navigate through the cycle.

**Market reaction:**
RBC shares responded positively to the Q3 results. The stock rose in early trading following the beat on both EPS and revenue. Analysts noted that the 18% ROE and strong capital generation position RBC favorably relative to Canadian bank peers.

**Analyst/notable quotes:**
